The Strait of Hormuz is a crucial, 21-mile-wide chokepoint connecting the oil-rich Persian Gulf to the open ocean, serving as the passage for roughly 20 percent of global oil and 25 percent of liquefied natural gas. Amid deep-rooted hostilities between the United States, Israel, and Iran, this strategic waterway represents a major global flashpoint. Positioned on the northern shore, Iran leverages its geographic advantage through asymmetric warfare capabilities—such as naval mines, speedboats, and missiles—threatening to disrupt shipping if attacked or heavily sanctioned. Although the US Fifth Fleet maintains a massive military presence to ensure the strait remains open, any direct confrontation risks an immediate blockade. Because existing alternative pipelines can handle only a fraction of the daily oil volume, a closure would cause crude prices to skyrocket overnight. This disruption would severely impact dependent Asian economies like China and India, hit vulnerable developing nations like Pakistan with immediate economic shock, and trigger a global recession.
