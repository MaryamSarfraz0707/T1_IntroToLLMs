The Strait of Hormuz, a 21-mile waterway between Iran and Oman, is the world's most critical energy chokepoint, handling 17–21 million barrels of oil daily (20% of global trade) and 25% of liquefied natural gas. Deep-rooted US-Iran hostilities escalated following the 1979 revolution, culminating in key events like the 2020 Soleimani assassination and the 2018 US withdrawal from the JCPOA nuclear deal. Israel's covert operations against Iran's nuclear program and the 2024 direct missile exchanges between the two nations heighten conflict risks. Iran wields enormous leverage through asymmetric tactics—mines, drones, speedboats—requiring minimal expense for maximum disruption. A closure would catastrophically spike oil prices from $80 to over $200 per barrel, devastating Asian economies (China, India, Japan, South Korea) and developing nations like Pakistan lacking strategic reserves. With no viable alternative routes capable of replacing the strait's capacity, the document emphasizes that only sustained diplomatic efforts and de-escalation can protect this vulnerable passage and prevent global economic collapse.
