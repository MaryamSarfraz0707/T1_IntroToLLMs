The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow 21-mile passage between Iran and Oman, is the world's most vital and vulnerable waterway, carrying roughly 20% of global oil trade and 25% of liquefied natural gas daily. Amid rising US-Israel-Iran tensions rooted in the 1979 revolution, the 2020 Soleimani killing, and the collapsed 2015 nuclear deal, Iran leverages its control over the strait as a powerful deterrent, threatening closure or using asymmetric tactics like mines, drones, and speedboat attacks. Israel's covert strikes on Iran's nuclear program and 2024 direct missile exchanges further heighten risks of disruption. A blockage could push oil prices from $80 to over $200 per barrel, severely impacting Asian economies like China, India, and Japan, and hitting vulnerable nations such as Pakistan hardest, since no alternative pipeline network can replace the strait's capacity. The US Navy's Fifth Fleet works to keep the passage open, but fragile diplomacy remains essential, as the document concludes that protecting this chokepoint requires negotiation and de-escalation rather than military force alone.
