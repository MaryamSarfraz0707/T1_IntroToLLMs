The Strait of Hormuz, a 21-mile narrow waterway between Iran and Oman, is crucial to the global economy, connecting the oil-rich Persian Gulf to the rest of the world. Approximately 20% of the world's oil and 25% of its liquefied natural gas pass through this strait daily, making it the world's most important waterway. Iran's strategic location and military presence along the strait give it significant power, allowing it to threaten closure and disrupt global markets. The ongoing US-Iran conflict, rooted in decades of hostility, has brought the strait to the forefront, with Iran's control over it being a key factor in any potential war scenario, and its closure could lead to a severe economic recession and global instability. Understanding the strait's importance is vital in today's tense geopolitical landscape.
